One winter weekend, two ways

Forty-eight hours in St. Catharines and the Benchlands, planned for a slow couple and planned for friends who want a night out.

Winter is the quiet season in St. Catharines and the Benchlands, and that is the point. The wineries have time to talk. The restaurants have tables. The lights are on in every window. Here is the same weekend planned two ways. [PLACEHOLDER: client to confirm names and hours.]

The slow way: wine country in the snow

Friday. Check in at Inn On The Twenty in Jordan Village before dark. Walk Main Street while the shop windows glow, then eat at the inn or book the tasting menu at Restaurant Pearl Morissette, Ontario's only two-MICHELIN-Star restaurant, ten minutes up the road.

Saturday. Sleep in. Breakfast at RPM Bakehouse, where the breads are made from heritage grains and the coffee is excellent. Spend the afternoon on the Beamsville Bench: a fireside tasting at Fielding Estates, icewine at [PLACEHOLDER winery], and the Escarpment view from Megalomaniac as the snow turns blue. Dinner is a short drive back to the village.

Sunday. A walk at Ball's Falls, where the two waterfalls freeze into columns, then a long lunch at a winery dining room before the drive home. Seventy-five minutes to Toronto.

The fast way: a downtown night out

Friday. Stay downtown in St. Catharines so nobody drives. Start with cocktails at Wandering Spirits, then dinner at Fat Rabbit or its louder little sibling Bar Les Incompétents, a Bib Gourmand with a raw bar worth fighting over.

Saturday. Brunch at OddBird, then the holiday shops on St. Paul Street and the Farmers Market for baking to take home. In the afternoon, skate at [PLACEHOLDER rink] under the lights. Catch a show at the FirstOntario Performing Arts Centre and finish with a late plate downtown.

Sunday. Drive twenty minutes to Port Dalhousie for a cold walk on the pier and a warm breakfast, then home.

Either way, you leave with a plan for next time. See where to eat and drink and where to stay.

Niagara Benchlands

Explore the stunning Niagara Benchlands, a haven for wine enthusiasts and nature lovers alike. With over 50 craft wineries dotting its steep slopes, orchards, and rural villages, this region boasts award-winning wines and breathtaking landscapes. Discover local markets, hidden shops, and rich history all amidst a food lover's paradise shaped by the unique climate and terroir of the area. Perched on the Niagara Escarpment, the Benchlands offer a wealth of natural wonders, including the renowned Bruce Trail and UNESCO Biosphere.

St. Catharines

Nestled in Ontario's picturesque Niagara Region, St. Catharines is a vibrant destination that perfectly blends culinary excellence, a thriving craft beverage scene, rich cultural experiences, and abundant outdoor activities. Savour farm-to-table delights at local restaurants, explore renowned wineries and craft breweries, and immerse yourself in the arts at theatres and galleries. Outdoor enthusiasts can enjoy scenic parks, hiking the Bruce Trail, and water sports in the historic waterfront neighbourhood of Port Dalhousie. Whether you're a foodie, wine lover, culture aficionado, or adventure seeker, St. Catharines offers something for everyone.

Questions, answered

Where is St. Catharines and the Benchlands?
St. Catharines and the Benchlands is the stretch of West Niagara between the Niagara Escarpment and Lake Ontario, anchored by St. Catharines and Beamsville, Vineland, Jordan and Port Dalhousie. St. Catharines is Niagara's urban side: restaurants, craft breweries, shops and live theatre in a walkable downtown. A few minutes away, the Niagara Benchlands hold Ontario's largest concentration of wineries, orchards and the UNESCO Niagara Escarpment Biosphere.
How far is St. Catharines and the Benchlands from Toronto?
About 1 hour 15 minutes on the QEW, or 90 minutes in holiday traffic. GO Transit runs train and bus service to St. Catharines, so you can leave the car at home. It is one of the easiest winter day trips from the city, and an easy place to stay the night.
How far is it from Niagara Falls?
St. Catharines is about 20 minutes from Niagara Falls, and Beamsville, Vineland and Jordan are about 30 minutes. Many visitors see the Falls lights, then come here to eat, drink and sleep away from the crowds.
What is there to do in St. Catharines and the Benchlands in winter?
Holiday light displays and decorated downtown streets, wineries pouring icewine by the fire, five MICHELIN Guide restaurants, holiday markets and Christmas-tree farms, winter walks on the Escarpment and the waterfront, skating, live theatre at the FirstOntario Performing Arts Centre, and warm shops in downtown St. Catharines and Jordan Village.
Where can we see holiday lights?
Downtown St. Catharines lights up St. Paul Street and Montebello Park for the season, and Port Dalhousie, Jordan Village and Beamsville decorate their main streets. Several wineries and farms run their own light displays. [PLACEHOLDER: confirm the 2026 displays and dates with the client.] See winter lights and trails for this year's list.
Can we go skating?
Yes. St. Catharines runs outdoor rinks when the weather allows, and indoor public skating runs all winter. [PLACEHOLDER: confirm rink names, hours and rental details.] See skating and snow.
What is icewine, and when is the icewine festival?
Icewine is made from grapes picked frozen on the vine, usually on the coldest nights of December and January, which is why Niagara makes more of it than anywhere else. Wineries across the Benchlands pour it all winter. The Twenty Valley Winter WineFest in Jordan Village and the Niagara Icewine Festival usually run in January. [PLACEHOLDER: confirm 2027 dates.]
Where should we stay?
Jordan Village has Inn On The Twenty, the boutique wine-country pick with a spa and restaurant. The Benchlands have winery guesthouses, farm stays and inns tucked into orchards. St. Catharines has the region's largest choice of hotels, all within 20 minutes of the wineries and the waterfront. See stay the night.
Is St. Catharines and the Benchlands good for families in winter?
Very. Light displays, tree farms, holiday markets, skating, easy snowy trails at Ball's Falls, the museums and galleries in St. Catharines, and family shows at the FirstOntario Performing Arts Centre. Most of it is free or nearly free. See winter outdoors and family fun.
