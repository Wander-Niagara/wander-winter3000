Winter is the easiest season to eat well in St. Catharines and the Benchlands. The tables that are booked out in October have room in December, the dining rooms are warm, and the wineries next door are pouring icewine. Niagara Benchlands and St. Catharines restaurants are continuing to win the hearts and stomachs of MICHELIN Guide Inspectors. When the MICHELIN Guide revealed its 2026 Toronto & Region findings this September, the list of accolades revealed some exciting new additions in St. Catharines and the Niagara Benchlands.

Bib Gourmand award winners, which MICHELIN considers to be standout restaurants serving some of the most exciting and creative food at a great value, were bestowed on only five new restaurants in total throughout Toronto and the surrounding area. St. Catharines' [Les Incompetents](https://www.barlesincompetents.ca/) and Jordan Village's [RPM Bakehouse](https://rpmbakehouse.com/) were the only award winners located outside of Toronto.

{% set imgSlotKey = "BLOG_MICHELIN_1" %}{% include "partials/img-slot.njk" %}

[Les Incompetents'](https://www.barlesincompetents.ca/) playful approach to breaking culinary rules caught the attention of MICHELIN Guide inspectors. Vintage vibes abound in this bustling space with cozy booths, a killer raw bar, a creative French-inspired menu, and elevated cocktails.

{% set imgSlotKey = "BLOG_MICHELIN_2" %}{% include "partials/img-slot.njk" %}

[RPM Bakehouse's](https://rpmbakehouse.com/) use of ingredients from the Restaurant Pearl Morissette farm and garden, and heritage grains in its slow-fermented breads, made it a standout. RPM Bakehouse offers excellent coffee, scratch-baked pastries, fresh plates from the garden, small bites and Pearl Morissette wines indoors or on its patio.

{% set imgSlotKey = "BLOG_MICHELIN_3" %}{% include "partials/img-slot.njk" %}

St. Catharines' [OddBird](https://oddbird.ca/index.php/menu-oddbird/) is another newly noted dining destination to gain MICHELIN attention. The beloved St. Paul Street bistro earned a recommendation as a "study of contrasts" that offers something for everyone, doesn't take itself too seriously, and still cooks with proper care and attention.

{% set imgSlotKey = "BLOG_MICHELIN_4" %}{% include "partials/img-slot.njk" %}

Jordan's [Restaurant Pearl Morissette](https://restaurantpearlmorissette.com/) held its title as Ontario's only restaurant with the honour of two MICHELIN Stars. Restaurant Pearl Morissette offers a fine-dining experience, showcasing an ever-changing menu of regional ingredients that reflects the European ideal of destination dining in the heart of agriculture. The indoor dining room, perched on the top floor of the Pearl Morissette winery, has floor-to-ceiling windows boasting views of the 42-acre property's regenerative farm. Guests can add wine pairings or non-alcoholic garden pairings to the ever-changing prix fixe menu.

{% set imgSlotKey = "BLOG_MICHELIN_5" %}{% include "partials/img-slot.njk" %}

In identifying "great food beyond Toronto proper" as a 2026 trend defining the city and surrounding region's gastronomy right now, the MICHELIN team also highlighted St. Catharines' [Fat Rabbit](https://fat-rabbit.ca/), which maintained its MICHELIN recommendation for the third year running. After adding a fabulous new dining space to accommodate even more guests at this unique restaurant and whole-animal butchery, Fat Rabbit Chef Zac Smith shows no signs of slowing down.

{% set imgSlotKey = "BLOG_MICHELIN_6" %}{% include "partials/img-slot.njk" %}

**Plan the winter weekend.** Book Pearl Morissette for Saturday night and stay in Jordan Village so nobody drives. Breakfast at RPM Bakehouse. Spend Sunday in St. Catharines: brunch at OddBird, the holiday shops on St. Paul Street, and an early dinner at Fat Rabbit or Les Incompétents before the drive home.
